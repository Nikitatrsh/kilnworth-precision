## The job

An orthopedic device startup (fictional for this demo) was moving a cannulated bone screw from prototype to its first commercial lot. The previous supplier turned the blank on one machine and milled the thread on another, which added handling marks and a second setup.

## What we did

- Ran the screw complete on a Swiss-type lathe with a thread-whirling attachment, from bar to finished part in one chucking.
- Gun-drilled the 0.051 in cannulation from both ends with a 0.002 in step allowance at the meet point.
- Measured the major diameter on a laser micrometer at the machine, every part, ±0.0002 in.
- Laser-marked the lot number, cleaned, and sent to our passivation partner with the paperwork for the device history record.

## Result

4,000 screws shipped in 25 working days. Cycle time went from 6.5 to 3.9 minutes per part, which brought the unit price down 31% at the same volume.
