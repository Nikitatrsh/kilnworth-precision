On a Swiss-type lathe the bar moves through a guide bushing and the tool cuts right next to it. The part never hangs out unsupported, so slender pins, bone screws, connector contacts and valve stems hold diameter and straightness that a conventional lathe cannot.

## What fits

Bar stock up to 1.25 in diameter. Parts up to 12 in long, most under 3 in.

## What we hold

- ±0.002 in standard on Swiss work.
- ±0.0002 in on diameters by agreement, measured on a laser micrometer at the machine.
- Threads to class 3A, thread milling and whirling for medical bone screw forms.

## Why it suits volume

Back-working spindles finish the cut-off end on the same machine, and bar loaders keep it running. Above about 500 pieces a Swiss part is usually cheaper than the same part from a mill-turn.
